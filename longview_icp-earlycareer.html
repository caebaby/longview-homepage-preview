The window that closes

The decisions you don't make in year one are the hardest ones to fix.

Three months into your first attending job, your student loan grace period ends. Your income is now 3–5x what it was. Your malpractice situation is new. Your tax filing is suddenly complicated. And every product-selling advisor in a 50-mile radius knows exactly when you signed your employment contract.

We built our early-career practice around one idea: meet physicians before the mistakes happen — not after. Because what you do in year one sets the floor for everything that follows.

Who this is for

Physicians in the first decade

Whether you're finishing training or a few years into practice, this is when the foundational decisions get made — and unmade.

01

The finishing resident or fellow

You're about to sign your first contract. Your loans are accruing. Your income is going to jump by a factor of five. The PSLF/IDR decision needs to happen before you start your first day — not after you've already made one year of wrong payments.

02

The early attending who started late

You've been practicing 2–4 years. You have income, maybe some investments, probably insurance you were sold during residency that you're not sure about. You know you're behind on retirement. Let's find out how far and build a real plan.

03

The new attending with sudden complexity

Multiple income streams. A practice entity. A spouse with income. Student loans you're trying to optimize. A lot of decisions landing at once. You need someone who's done this specific thing a hundred times.

What we focus on

Six areas that move the needle most

Early-career physicians face a specific set of decisions. We've built our practice around getting these right.

Student Loan Strategy

PSLF, IDR, SAVE, refinancing — this single decision is often worth substantial money. The rules changed. If your strategy is more than a year old, it needs to be rebuilt.

Most urgent right now — 6-12 month window
First-Year Cash Flow Plan

Your income tripled overnight. We build a spending plan that protects your future without making you feel like you're living in residency.

Disability & Life Insurance

Physicians are dramatically underinsured at this stage. We'll tell you exactly what you need, what you were sold that you don't need, and where to get coverage that actually fits.

Investment Account Sequencing

401(k), Roth IRA, backdoor Roth, HSA — in what order, and how much. Most new attendings have the right accounts open in the wrong order with the wrong investments.

Tax Planning for the Income Surge

Filing is not planning. Your first year as an attending is the most important tax year of your career. We build a proactive strategy before April, not a documentation exercise after.

Estate & Beneficiary Basics

Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts, basic will structure, disability-of-a-parent contingency. Skipped by 90% of new attendings. Costs real money when discovered late.

Common questions

Things we hear all the time

"I'm still in residency — I can't afford a financial planner yet."

The PSLF/SAVE decision alone is a substantial financial choice that has to be made now, not later. The cost of advice at this stage is often smaller than the cost of the wrong decision made without it. We work with residents specifically because of this.

"I'll figure it out once I start making real money."

The decisions you make in your first 90 days as an attending set the baseline for everything. Most are irreversible. The income is real the day you sign — the planning needs to be there the day before.

"You've been reading internet blogs for six months and still haven't done anything."

Great start — genuinely. We've reviewed hundreds of physician plans from people who said the same thing. The blogs get most things right. They can't account for your specific situation, your loans, your contract, your spouse's income. That's what we do.

"How do I know you won't try to sell me something?"

Operating under a fiduciary standard when acting in an advisory capacity, we're required to act in your best interest. Our advisory fee is structured to be consistent regardless of what you buy or invest in. Check our disclosures — it's in writing, not a talking point.
