Why I left banking to practice as a fiduciary
“

Twenty years in banking taught me exactly how financial institutions make money. And once you see that clearly enough, you can't unsee it. The products, the incentives, the advice that isn't quite advice — I knew the model from the inside. That's exactly why I chose to leave it.

I came to financial planning in 2010 — later than most advisors. But I brought something most advisors don't have: two decades of watching how financial products actually work from the inside of a bank. The banking world isn't built around clients. It's built around products. When I finally made the transition, I wanted to practice the only way that made sense to me: fiduciary, and built entirely around the client's interest.

Based in Tupelo, MS, I serve clients locally and nationwide. The physicians and dentists I work with don't need someone to sell them something. They need a planner who will tell them the honest truth about where they stand — and then help them build from there.

What I focus on

Areas I spend the most time in

Student Loan Strategy
PSLF optimization, IDR strategy, and navigating the fallout of the SAVE plan collapse. The right path depends on your employment, income, and timeline — not generic advice.
Work-Optional Retirement Planning
Building the number that gives you the option to stop — without forcing you to quit medicine to get there. For physicians who want choice, not a mandatory exit date.
Tax Strategy for High-Income Physicians
Proactive tax planning — not just filing. Backdoor Roth, solo 401(k), QBI deduction for practice owners, and the strategies most CPAs miss when they're just doing compliance work.
Investment Management
Evidence-based, low-cost portfolios. No proprietary products, no active management theater. Built around your actual time horizon and risk tolerance — not a sales agenda.
Insurance Review
Disability insurance (own-occupation), life insurance — and identifying the policies you may have been sold in residency that are costing you money every month without serving your interests.
Physician Family Financial Planning
Estate basics, beneficiary alignment, education funding, and making sure the plan actually reflects your family — not a generic client profile. Including dual-physician households.

About Louis

A planner who came from the other side of the table

W. Louis Holmes spent twenty years inside the banking world before transitioning to fiduciary financial planning in 2010. That background is the foundation of everything he does — he's seen firsthand how financial institutions are structured, how products are designed, and where the incentives point. It's exactly why he chose a different path.

Louis earned his MBA from Ole Miss in 1989 and has built his practice around long-term client relationships and the quality of work that sustains them. He serves clients from the Tupelo, MS area and works with physicians and dentists nationwide. His approach is unhurried, relationship-first, and grounded in the belief that honest counsel is one of the highest forms of service.

Faith & Community
Louis serves as an elder in his church and is deeply embedded in the Tupelo, MS community. His faith informs his approach to client relationships — he builds for the long term, doesn't rush decisions, and believes that honest counsel is a form of service.
