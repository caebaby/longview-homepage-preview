The number you're not looking at

High income and a good financial plan are not the same thing.

The physicians we work with at this stage have often done something right. They've saved consistently. They have investments. They carry less debt. What they don't have is a comprehensive strategy that ties all of it together — across accounts, across taxes, across the practice, across what happens when they're ready to stop.

We built our work with established physicians around one question: Not "how do I grow my wealth?" — but "when can I stop, and what does it cost to get there faster?"

What we focus on

Six areas that matter at this stage

Comprehensive planning for physicians with complex financial pictures — not a one-size approach.

Investment Coordination

Multiple accounts (old 403b, IRAs, brokerage, practice retirement). We consolidate the picture, align the allocation, and fix the tax-location strategy across everything you own.

Tax Planning

High earners at this stage are often over-paying substantial amounts in taxes each year. Practice entity structure, deferred comp, backdoor Roth, Qualified Business Income — year-round, not April.

Work-Optional Modeling

We give you a number and a timeline. Not vague — specific. "At your current rate, you can practice on your own terms by age [X]." Then we show you the 3 things that move that date.

Practice & Partnership Decisions

Partnership buy-in, PE acquisition offers, selling the practice, transitioning ownership. We model the scenarios that matter financially before you sign anything.

Estate & Legacy Planning

Beneficiary alignment, trust structure, titling, gifting. Often untouched for 10+ years even in households with significant assets. The gaps are expensive to discover late.

Insurance Right-Sizing

At this stage, most attending physicians are under-insured on both life and disability. We audit what you have and give you an honest answer about what you actually need.

Common questions

"I already have an advisor."

Many physicians at this stage do. The question isn't whether you have an advisor — it's whether they've done comprehensive work across all six areas. "I'm too busy to go through this whole process."

One call. 30 minutes. We'll tell you where the gaps are and whether it's worth a deeper look. If it's not, you've spent 30 minutes and learned something useful. We've built our practice around respect for physician time.

"I've been burned by advisors before."

That's a common thing we hear from physicians at this stage. Operating under a fiduciary standard when acting in an advisory capacity, our compensation is structured to be consistent regardless of what you buy or invest in. Check our disclosures. It's in writing, not a talking point.

"I'm close to retirement — is it too late to make real changes?"
